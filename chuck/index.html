Chuck!

Explore and survive these terrifying lands, and uncover the mystery of the Skult. With your companion Crow as your main weapon, charge into the horde of the night and save the souls of the damned with this 90s and early 2000s inspired Halloween shooter..

Overview

  • A 3rd Person creature collector about digging up monster remains from graveyards, battling other people in this world, and leveling up your monsters and yourself as their leader. The player can collect up to X unique monsters and create teams of up to 5 monsters, using 3 in battle, and the other 2 for passive effects during battle. Uncover the mystery of your awakening as Chuck the skeleton, whose family’s grave has just been robbed. Help Chuck find his wife and son, to bring them home.

The Story

  • So the story about Chuck! is pretty interesting. The game doesn't really exist... kinda.
  • Chuck started as a sketch I made about 2 years ago in the margins of margins on a corner of a page in my notebook. I kept drawing him more and more, and what started off as a generic skeleton, turned into a pretty goofy and fun character. I started imagining how he would move; walking, running, doing certain things. I legitamately started going through the character design process without really knowing it. I wanted to know more about this character I made, and the only way to find the answer was to make it.
  • So I made a list. I figured if I was going to make this character, I should really go all out. I listed all the things I wanted this character to do, and the systems that would allow that to happen in the rig and topology. I spent about a week working on the model. And about a month working on the rig (between school and work).
  • While working on the rig, I figured I should develop some tools to make this process easier, and future processes as well. I made a tool that allows me to make ribbons much easier, and a couple more smaller scripts to hasten up my workflow. However, I wasn't necessarily satisfied with the character.
  • You see when I drew Chuck in my notebook, he had a boring straw hat. When I modeled this hat, Chuck still felt a little like a boring generic skeleton with some clothes. So I started asking some questions to myself.
  • "With the hat being the only part of the character not designed, what can I do to bring it home?"
  • "How does a hat make a design feel complete?"
  • "What kind of hat would look best for Chuck?"
  • It was then that I came up with the idea for a secondary character, Hatty. A sentient living tophat with a sash that acts as the mouth, with 2 eyes that pop up out from underneath. I ran the design by a couple of people, and they really felt it was a sweet idea. They liked the contrast between a goofy skeleton, and a somehwat sinister looking hat. So, I got to work.
  • And work it was, about another month of work on just the hat alone, but it was so worth it. I learned so much about ribbings, not just being in a straight line, certain types of spaces switching, getting comfortable in the node editor, and shrinkwrapping certain components.
  • While developing Chuck, I had a north start in mind while doing the rig, and that would be "The Food Thief" by Mindbender. I watched that breakdown over and over and over. That entire rig, made with only the base tools available in Maya. But I wanted to take it a step further; I wanted to put it into a game, and make it a game ready asset.
  • And this is where Chuck just goes from being a character I saw through, to a game design doc with a concept album and plenty of concept art for. I went hard for this idea, and I loved every second of it. I created design principles, a whole ass narrative. I wrote backstories for the characters and the world itself, a whole mythology.
  • Soon after, I created a gameplay prototype inspired by Fossil Fighters and rhythm games, and thats where I sort of left it. I want to make this a full fledge game, but I need to work out the flow and architecture just a little bit on paper before I feel I'm confident enough to actually tackle it.

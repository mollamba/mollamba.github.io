500 Finger Fillet

Explore and survive these terrifying lands, and uncover the mystery of the Skult. With your companion Crow as your main weapon, charge into the horde of the night and save the souls of the damned with this 90s and early 2000s inspired Halloween shooter..

Overview

  • 500 STABS. 20 MINUTES TO ESCAPE. A horror roguelite about stabbing yourself in the hand 500 times to escape a deadly trap. Can you make it out with all your fingers? Or will you bleed out on the table?

The Story

  • The story of 500FF actually begins around the end of 2025. My friend Prasin had forwarded me a job posting on one of the Philly Game Dev discord servers, from a woman named Heather Flowers. I had never heard of her before, but he said that I should message her anyway.
  • I remember being incredibly nervous. Everyone on my senior project team apparently knew who she was, and the games she had made. I remember my friend Ako freaking out about the prospect of me getting to work with her, and It made me EVEN MORE nervous. However I had nothing to worry about. Heather was (and still is) incredibly nice, and pretty clear about what she was looking for.
  • An art test. This would actually end up becoming my send art test I would ever do (up to that point). She was very clear about what she was looking for, and opened the door up to conversation if I had any questions.
  • I got to work immediately
  • I think I actually ended up finishing the test in about a couple of days of hard work. I wasn't working all day on them, but a decent amount of time on just 1 asset. A severed finger, modeled, rigged, textured and animated. I submitted my deliverables and waited.
  • The new year passed and I ended up getting the gig! I was super pumped, but again super nervous. I would be the project's artist, making assets from concept to polish. I of course had a great support system behind me, one that I am greatly thankful for to this day: My family, girlfriend, and Heather of course helped me get accustomed. I got to meet the team, Josie, Surasshu, and eventually Mickey, and we all got working hard on 500FF.
  • Week by week, I was learning new things about our team as well as legitamate development. The team operated out of a discord server, and we would all regularly post our updates for feedback and whatnot. Its a good system for a team thats remote and pretty small.
  • Development was going smoothly. I don't remember a single time I felt 'crunched' or forced to work more than I was comfortable with. There was one moment though I did feel a small fire under my ass.
  • It was some time after the demo, and I was playing the game one night, and I felt the game could use a little more. I ended up writing down a whole list of things that could be improved. I tried to play and look at the game, not as its developer, but as a gamer. How would I feel about looking and playing this game for the first time?
  • A complete overhaul of the games visuals, and a total redesign of the character at the other end of the table, Ursa.
  • Its kinda funny to me, Ursa as a character contained a certain attitude that made it easy to want to work on her, and the game surrounding her. Once we found a sort of voice for her, everything else pretty much came easily. I started playing a whole bunch of other low poly pixelated 3D games to kind of understand what they were doing, and took notes on everything, literal pages! Then it all started to make sense.
  • It was one day that I was mowing the lawn, that I thought through the process for a new knife we had concepted; the scalpel. More specifically, I was mapping out how I would texture it. Texturing up to this point was for sure my biggest hurdle. It was what I had the least amount of experience in, and least amount of confidence in. But there was something in the air, the team was on what felt like a hotstreak, and especially after just getting done making Ursa, I was actually able to do that weird artist thing where you make the thing exactly how you imagined it. And to this day, Its one of the best looking knives.
  • It was actually kinda comical if I'm being honest. When Heather told me about all the knives and rings we would need for the upcoming demo, she SEEMED like she was worried about the workload I would have to do in a relatively short time. But there was no worry from me. I was having a blast making new things and my newfound confidence in texturing in this style did a lot for me.
  • So here we are, at the time of writing, we are about 3 ish weeks from release. Development went incredibly well, and I sincerely could not be happier that I applied back almost a year ago.

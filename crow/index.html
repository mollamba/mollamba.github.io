CrowHallow

view on steam

Explore and survive these terrifying lands, and uncover the mystery of the Skult. With your companion Crow as your main weapon, charge into the horde of the night and save the souls of the damned with this 90s and early 2000s inspired Halloween shooter..

Overview

  • CrowHallow started as a small dream game of mine. I had always known I wanted to make a first person shooter in a low poly aesthetic. So when the chance presented itself, I took the shot with everything I had.
  • With a 1 year development cycle, we set out to create a wave based FPS where you have a crow instead of a gun. We started development HOT in October, meetings every week while still being full time students. By the end of the year, we had a pretty good prototype working, and some enemies too.
  • My role during dev was design lead and 3D artist, creating the models, rigs, and animations for the characters and most of the environment. Concept art was a community effort from the team, allowing a lot of different ideas to be shared, which genuinely led to some of my favorite designs in the game.
  • In the end, we went over our development time by about a month. And while the game did not come out perfect, it has opened many doors and started a plethora of conversations amongst the team and other developers when networking for us who worked on the game.

The Story

  • It was August of 2024, and Saphead Studios had released its first game; "Galactic-A-Tactic"...
  • ...and there was much rejoicing...
  • I was the games character artist. One of our other team members Michael took care of the concept art, and then I would turn it into a fully fucntional character. In almost every way, it was my first fore in being a character artist, and technical animation.
  • This is to say, our next project would be a true test.
  • So,
  • This is all to say, in August, after the teams first release, we were looking for our next project. Almost everyone in the team had a pitch.
  • There were a bunch of good ideas floating around the team, and when voting time came, it was wittled down to 2 choices. A tie.
  • Aki's game pitch, "Flipadelphia" we called it, was a Jet Set Radio / Sunset Overdrive type game where you skate around a smaller, more iconic version of Philadelphia figthing different factions and doing tricks. Almost the whole team loved the idea; a game with style, challenging enogh dev wise that it would look good on a portfolio.
  • The other game was mine, a game called "Crowhalla", a medieval fantasy fps inspired by og CoD Zombies and Devil Daggers. When push came to shove, the team felt that my game was more feasible with a 1 year deadline.
  • We wasted no time getting into development. I was thrown the reigns of directing the team. We started on gameplay prototypes and flowcharts, mocking up game systems using Miro board. We had multiple rounds of concept art going every week, which we then would vote on and rework designs.
  • Our first couple months of development came and went, then the new year started. And with a new year, also meant a new school term. The game had a working prototype and gameloop, but no real level or driving narative.
  • Individuals were getting busy, myself included. And the work load slowly dwindled and dwindled withing the team, and eventually some members would have their last peice of work during this time. We were entering our Junior year, which was notorious for being brutal. Not to mention some of us were on internships and working full time. And before we knew it, it was March, and I had started my internship, along with a couple other members in the team.
  • The project was left to myself and 2 others on the team. And from March to July, the 3 of us put everything we had into the game. We reworked the environment. We developed a story. All Enemies and AI were implemented, and so much more was done. We had been going so hard for so long, that when July hit, we were burnt out like waxless candles.
  • We were able to finally to pick ourselves up, after much deliberating about ourselves and where we were. We scoped down, cut a lot of stuff, and finished the game just over schedule. The following year, we were able to take CrowHallow on our back to California to use as networking. And while our story is still being written, it was truly one of the most important things we could have done for ourselves.
